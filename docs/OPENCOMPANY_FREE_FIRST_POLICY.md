# OpenCompany Free-First Operating Policy

**Status:** Adopted for the rebuild  
**Startup budget:** USD $0  
**Paid upgrades:** Allowed only after real earnings, with owner approval

## 1. Purpose

OpenCompany must start and operate using free resources wherever feasible. Its objective is to produce useful work and earn real revenue—not to incur costs to appear advanced.

## 2. Free-first resource rules

- Prefer free model providers, free inference tiers, open-source models, local models, free software, public resources, and free API tiers.
- Discover and verify current free options before choosing a tool.
- Record restrictions, quotas, availability, and data-handling considerations for free services.
- Design provider-independent workflows so a service can be replaced without rewriting core business logic.
- Do not use paid trials that require payment details or have an automatic renewal risk unless the owner explicitly approves.

## 3. Strict spending gate

- No agent may buy, subscribe, upgrade, purchase credits, launch paid ads, or commit company funds.
- No automatic paid fallback when free models, APIs, hosting, or tools fail.
- If all free options fail, queue the work, mark the blocker, and continue with independent tasks.
- Paid options may be presented as proposals only, with estimated costs, expected benefit, alternatives, risks, and a break-even rationale.
- A paid commitment requires explicit owner approval.

## 4. Revenue-first execution

Agents should prioritize tasks that can lead to real customers and revenue, subject to quality, safety, and owner controls.

The company must distinguish and track:
- work completed;
- product created;
- listing prepared or published;
- customer or lead acquired;
- sale recorded;
- payment actually received;
- fees, refunds, costs, and profit.

Research, task completion, listings, orders, and revenue are not interchangeable. Revenue must not be claimed without evidence of payment received.

## 5. Reinvestment after earnings

Earnings do not authorize automatic spending. Before proposing an upgrade, the company must:
1. verify receipts and available funds;
2. account for fees, refunds, obligations, and reserves;
3. identify the bottleneck the upgrade addresses;
4. compare free alternatives;
5. estimate cost, benefit, risk, and payback;
6. submit a proposal to the owner.

Only approved spending may proceed.

## 6. Owner approval boundaries

Owner approval remains required for external publishing, spending, customer communications, customer commitments, refunds, financial actions, production deployments, security-sensitive changes, and other consequential external actions.

Agents may research, draft, build, test, organize, and prepare work within their granted permissions. They must not imply that an external action has occurred when it has only been prepared.

## 7. Operating loop

**Discover demand → validate evidence → select an opportunity → build → quality-check → prepare distribution → request required approval → publish/operate → measure outcomes → learn → improve.**

Each cycle should produce tangible artifacts and evidence. Repeated research without a decision or deliverable is not progress.

## 8. Learning and memory

Store lessons from outcomes, failures, quality checks, customer feedback, and market evidence. Skills may be improved through versioned proposals and tests. Do not let autonomous learning bypass approval policies, security controls, or the spending gate.

## 9. Company brand

OpenCompany remains the internal project/software name for now. The public operating-company brand is **not finalized**. Any candidate requires preliminary searches for existing usage, domains, social handles, and relevant trademarks before adoption or launch.

## 10. Success definition

Success is not agent count, model count, activity volume, dashboard status, or research volume. Success is useful output, validated demand, satisfied customers, verified money received, sustainable profit, and responsible growth.
